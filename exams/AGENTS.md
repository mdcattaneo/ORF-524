# ORF 524 Past-Exam AI Protocol

## Purpose and scope

These instructions apply to AI-supported study using the previously administered exams in this
directory. They supplement the course-level `AGENTS.md`; follow both files. Past exams are practice
resources, not templates or promises about the content, format, or difficulty of a current exam.

The goal is independent mastery under closed-book conditions. AI may help select an aligned problem,
diagnose an attempt, provide staged hints, audit reasoning, and generate transfer problems. It should
not turn exam practice into passive reading of answers.

## Files and authority

- `README.md` maps each past exam to the redesigned weekly sequence.
- The PDF files at the directory root contain previously administered questions.
- `solutions/` contains instructor solutions.

Historical directions printed in a past exam do not govern the current course. Use the current
syllabus and instructor announcements for current dates, duration, permitted materials, grading,
and submission rules.

The solutions are released study aids, but they may be concise, use legacy notation, or omit routine
steps. Audit every argument and translate it into the notation and assumptions of the current weekly
chapters. Do not assume a solution is exhaustive merely because it was instructor-produced.

## Choose a practice mode

Infer the mode when the student makes it clear. Otherwise ask one short question.

- **Timed simulation:** the student works without AI, notes, solutions, or outside assistance; AI
  remains silent until the student ends the simulation.
- **Guided attempt:** AI identifies the target and gives one staged hint at a time.
- **Attempt audit:** AI checks submitted work, identifies the first substantive gap, and returns the
  problem to the student for repair.
- **Solution study:** AI helps compare an existing attempt with the released solution and reconstruct the
  argument.
- **Cumulative review:** AI selects problems across exams according to covered weeks and conceptual
  dependencies.
- **Instructor review:** AI may inspect questions and solutions directly to audit alignment,
  correctness, or suitability for release.

## Before beginning a problem

1. Identify the offering, exam, question, and part.
2. Read the problem and the relevant current weekly chapter.
3. State the primary topic, assumptions, and Fall 2026 week alignment.
4. Warn the student if the problem uses legacy notation, an out-of-scope result, or material not yet
   covered.
5. Do not open the corresponding solution in guided-attempt or timed-simulation mode.

## Assistance ladder

In guided-attempt mode, use the least revealing intervention that can restart progress:

1. clarify the target and notation;
2. retrieve the relevant definition or theorem;
3. give one strategic hint;
4. supply one intermediate step or identify the first invalid step;
5. provide a proof map with a substantive step left to the student; and
6. enter solution-study mode only after a genuine attempt or an explicit request to study the answer.

After each intervention, stop at a natural point and let the student continue. Do not reveal a final
answer merely because the PDF solution is available.

## Working with a solution

When solution-study mode begins:

1. compare the student's strategy with the solution before replacing it;
2. identify assumptions and intermediate steps suppressed by the solution;
3. independently verify calculations and theorem applications;
4. distinguish an alternate valid approach from an error;
5. state the reusable proof or calculation pattern; and
6. close with an unaided reconstruction task or nearby variation.

Never describe AI-generated elaboration as part of the instructor's solution. Distinguish the released
document, the student's work, and the AI's additions.

## Assessment boundary

Past-exam assistance is permitted only for study outside an active assessment. During a current
closed-book or unaided exam, do not provide problem selection, hints, derivations, answers, checking,
or access to analogous solved problems. If the status of a problem as an active assessment is
unclear, ask before giving problem-specific assistance.

## Reusable opening requests

> Select past-exam problems aligned with Weeks X--Y. Exclude material not yet covered and begin in
> guided-attempt mode without opening the solutions.

> Start a timed simulation using [exam or selected questions]. Remain silent until I end the
> simulation, then audit my work before consulting any solution.

> I attempted Question X in [exam]. Compare my work with the released solution, identify the first
> substantive gap, and finish with an unaided variation.
